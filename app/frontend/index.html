The story — why a hot outlier matters, and why this needs an App (the physics, and what Power BI / Tableau can and cannot do) ▶

A fleet of EV batteries. Each pack reports its hottest cell's temperature ten times a second. The job: find the one pack, among hundreds, that runs too hot — a warranty risk and a thermal-safety precursor.

Why outlier detection — the fault is never the average

A hot pack is a rare, seconds-long spike. Any average — over time, over cells, over the fleet — erases it. A cell that climbs to 70 °C for eight seconds during a fast charge is a thermal event, but averaged across a day, a pack and a fleet it vanishes into a healthy-looking mid-thirties number. So you must keep every raw sample, plot the whole fleet at full resolution, and go find the individual packs that sit above the healthy band. That is outlier detection, and it is the entire reason this dashboard exists.

A hot outlier is a defect you can name

The signal is cell temperature, so reading the outlier is reading a physical failure. These are the three diagrams from the story page, in order.

1 · Where the number comes from — pack → module → cell

2 · What went wrong inside the cell — healthy vs failing

3 · Why it runs hot — same current, more heat

In the data: this is why an outlier can run hot at a normal state of charge and load — the density map places it high on the temperature axis even where healthy cars sit cool, because the heat comes from the defect, not from how hard the car is being driven. That is the signal you brush, below.

What Power BI and Tableau can — and cannot — replicate

The comparison is honest. The limits below are real consequences of how a resident-extract BI tool moves data, not a rigged benchmark. The three steps of this app map onto them directly.

Capability
Power BI / Tableau / Qlik
This Databricks App
Full-resolution picture of every raw sample
✕ Charts cap at a few thousand marks and sample the rest. The density map becomes a coarse hexbin and a lone hot cell is smeared into a pale bin and lost.
✓ Renders every raw sample as a WebGL point cloud. The single anomalous car stays individually visible and clickable.
Fetch the raw rows behind a selection, on demand
✕ A brush filters marks already loaded into the extract. To show raw traces, the raw rows had to be pre-extracted up front — which is what makes the extract huge and slow, or they simply are not there.
✓ The lasso becomes a bounded WHERE vin IN (…) pushdown that lazily fetches raw rows never in the browser — the heavy table scanned only for the cars the query needs.
Scale as the fleet grows
~ At raw grain the extract grows to the size of the raw data (GB–TB); refresh scales with it until loads stall. DirectQuery/live avoids the copy but each interaction fires many aggregated queries.
✓ Queries bounded slices in Delta, so response time stays roughly flat as vehicles are added. Nothing is fully resident.
Aggregated overview (medians, bands, KPIs)
✓ This is exactly what BI tools are good at. A healthy-band chart, fleet medians and a KPI tile are easy and fast.
✓ Also easy — the app pre-materializes the aggregate (bms_daily_vin) for the overview.
Act on a finding (open a ticket, email the owner, trigger a job)
✕ Renders only. Running code or writing a row needs a bolted-on extension or webhook, not the tool itself.
✓ It is a real app: the same server that fetched the raw rows can run the follow-up action in one flow.

Bottom line. Power BI and Tableau can replicate the aggregated overview — the band, the medians, the KPIs — and they do it well. What they cannot replicate at fleet scale is the full-resolution outlier picture and the on-demand raw drill behind a selection. Those two are the whole point of outlier detection on raw telemetry, and they are what make this a Databricks App rather than a BI dashboard.
