Databricks demo · visualizing massive data

Why Databricks Apps

For interactive visualization of massive data, a Databricks App does what classic BI tools like Power BI, Tableau and Qlik cannot. The example here is a fleet of EV batteries — the point is general.

The reasons

Five reasons it’s a Databricks App

Full fidelity

BI charts cap at a few thousand marks and sample the rest, so rare outliers vanish. The App renders every raw sample as a full-resolution picture.

Scales with the fleet

Add more vehicles and a BI extract keeps growing until loads and refreshes stall. The App queries bounded slices in the lakehouse, so response time stays flat as the fleet grows.

One platform to maintain

A separate BI tool is another system to license, secure, refresh and govern beside the lakehouse. The App runs on the data platform you already have — one place, one copy, one story.

Licensing you can save

Per-seat BI licenses recur for every viewer. A Databricks App serves users on compute you already run, avoiding a separate dashboarding subscription.

Interaction BI can’t do

It’s a real app: a lasso that triggers a server-side raw fetch, a sample-size control, a drill to one vehicle’s complete raw signal — none of it resident in the browser.

To see and interrogate massive data at full fidelity — not just chart a summary — a Databricks App is the right tool; a resident-extract BI tool is the wrong shape.

The outlier in the pack

The example that carries this demo: a fleet of EV batteries. Each pack reports its hottest cell’s temperature ten times a second. The job — find the one pack, among hundreds, that runs too hot.

What happened to the battery

A hot outlier is a defect you can name

The signal is cell temperature — so reading the outlier is reading a physical failure.

1 · Where the number comes from — pack → module → cell

In the data: this is cell_temp_max on the Y axis of every plot. A single failing cell, buried among thousands, is what pushes it above the healthy band.

2 · What went wrong inside the cell — healthy vs failing

In the data: that hot spot is the seconds-long temperature spike you see in Step 2’s overlaid curves and Step 3’s detailed trace. It repeats every time the pack is worked hard.

3 · Why it runs hot — same current, more heat

In the data: this is why an outlier can run hot at a normal state of charge and load — the density map (Step 1) places it high on the temperature axis even where healthy cars sit cool, because the heat comes from the defect, not from how hard the car is being driven.

Raw-sample density — state of charge × cell temperature

MIDDLE PLOT + TABLE · the sampled suspects

Overlaid raw curves + a forensic table

The rectangle becomes a query. It takes the N hottest vehicles in the region and fetches their raw temperature-vs-time traces on demand — overlaid, the failing pack’s line peels upward in repeating spikes. Beside them, a table lists each sampled vehicle with its peak temperature, seconds spent hot, mileage and lowest voltage — sortable evidence, worst first.

↓   click a row in the table
BOTTOM PLOT · one vehicle in full

That vehicle’s complete raw signal

Click a suspect and the dashboard fetches every raw sample for that one vehicle — a hundred thousand points — and draws its full-resolution trace: the exact shape of each thermal excursion, when it happened, how long it lasted, how the pack recovered. This is the evidence for a warranty claim or a field-quality report.

At no point is the raw signal thrown away. That is what makes the outlier findable — and, as the next tab explains, it is exactly what a resident-extract BI tool cannot do at fleet scale.

Tool comparison · Databricks Apps vs classic BI

Why this only works on Databricks Apps

The workflow needs two things at once: a full-resolution picture of hundreds of millions of raw samples, and the raw rows behind any selection on demand. Classic BI can’t do both at scale.

The root difference

Where the data lives decides what the tool can do

Tableau, Power BI and Qlik keep data resident (an extract / imported model) and render only a few thousand marks. The Databricks App keeps data in the lakehouse and fetches only what each step needs.

Point cap plus line aggregation. A true raw overlay is not possible.

Qlik~

The raw points must be in the in-memory model first, and the chart still caps them.

The picture in one chart

Why massive data breaks classic BI visuals

A chart renders only so many marks, and a BI tool must load the data first — so as the dataset grows, the fraction it can show shrinks, and it fills the gap by summarizing.

Two things break: rendering (no chart draws a billion marks) and residency (the loaded copy grows with the data). The App avoids both — it keeps data in the lakehouse and moves only a bounded, already-shaped result to the screen.
